Why SororIT exists

As a result, many move forward alone, not knowing that others, elsewhere, are asking the same questions, doubting in the same way, or have already found answers that could help.

SororIT isn't here to make up for a lack of skill, women don't lack skill. It answers a different need: a space to grow in a field where you're outnumbered, without having to justify yourself, compare yourself, or prove twice as much. A place where every path, every question, every small win belongs.

We don't just want to hold our ground in this field. We want to truly thrive in it, and that's easier to build together.

Curious about tech? It's never too late to start.

"Tech" isn't just code. It's networks, data, product, cybersecurity, hardware, research, and a dozen fields you haven't discovered yet. You don't need to know which one is yours before you start, explore and see where you recognize yourself.

Our design choices

Every choice here serves one goal: encourage without comparing.

Grounded in research, not just good intentions. Studies around Instagram's like-count experiment found that publicly visible metrics turn posting into a competition and intensify social comparison, a pattern researchers connect to anxiety and lower self-esteem, especially for women. Research on healthy online communities also points to what actually builds belonging: moderation, mutual exchange, and celebrating members' wins, not follower counts. So on SororIT, likes, comments and followers stay uncounted, on purpose.

We also looked at what makes people want to come back without it turning unhealthy. Psychology research on sustained motivation (Self-Determination Theory) finds that people stay engaged long-term when three needs are met, autonomy, competence, and relatedness, not when they're pulled back by streaks, pressure, or fear of missing out. That shaped these choices:

Source: Instagram's hidden-likes test (2019); Verduyn et al., 2017, Social Issues and Policy Review.

Autonomy

No forced notifications or guilt-tripping reminders. You choose if and when you hear from SororIT.

Competence

Private, meaningful feedback instead of a public score to chase or fall behind on.

Relatedness

Reciprocal replies and mentorship, not a follower count, connection is the point, not the audience size.

Questions you might have

Short answers about how SororIT is meant to work.

Who can join?

Women and girls interested in tech, at any level, curious beginners, students, career changers and professionals.

How do posts get seen without likes?

You can like a post, only its author can see how many likes it has received, nobody else can.

How is it kept safe?

Comments are moderated, any post can be reported in one click, and private messages only open after the other person accepts.

Is this the real platform?

Not yet. It's just a mockup. Leave your email and we'll tell you when it launches.
