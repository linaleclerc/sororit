Why SororIT exists

In tech, women are still a minority. As a result, many move forward alone, not knowing that others, elsewhere, are asking the same questions, doubting in the same way, or have already found answers that could help.

SororIT doesn't exist to make up for a lack of skill — there isn't one. It exists because thriving in a field where you're outnumbered needs a space where you can talk without justifying yourself, comparing yourself, or proving twice as much. A place where every path, every question, every small win belongs.

We don't just want to hold our ground in this field. We want to truly thrive in it — and that's easier to build together.

Curious about tech? It's never too late to start.

"Tech" isn't just code. It's design, data, product, cybersecurity, hardware, research, and a dozen fields you haven't discovered yet. You don't need to know which one is yours before you start — scroll the feed and see where you recognize yourself.

Our design choices

Every choice here serves one goal: encourage without comparing.

Grounded in research, not just good intentions. Studies around Instagram's like-count experiment found that publicly visible metrics turn posting into a competition and intensify social comparison — a pattern researchers connect to anxiety and lower self-esteem, especially for women. Research on healthy online communities also points to what actually builds belonging: moderation, reciprocal replies, and celebrating members' wins — not follower counts. So on SororIT, likes, comments and followers stay uncounted, on purpose.

We also looked at what makes people want to come back without it turning unhealthy. Psychology research on sustained motivation (Self-Determination Theory) finds that people stay engaged long-term when three needs are met — autonomy, competence, and relatedness — not when they're pulled back by streaks, pressure, or fear of missing out. That shaped these choices:

Sources: reporting on Instagram's hidden-likes test (2019) · research on social comparison theory and social media use · Deci & Ryan, Self-Determination Theory

Autonomy

No forced notifications or guilt-tripping reminders. You choose if and when you hear from SororIT.

Competence

Private, meaningful feedback ("this helped someone") instead of a public score to chase or fall behind on.

Relatedness

Real replies and mentorship, not a follower count — connection is the point, not the audience size.

Questions you might have

Short answers about how SororIT is meant to work.

Who can join?

Women and girls interested in tech, at any level — curious beginners, students, career changers and professionals. Mentors are reviewed by the team before they appear.

How do posts get seen without likes?

The feed shows recent posts and the topics you follow. Instead of public counts, authors get a private note when their post helped someone.

How is it kept safe?

Comments are moderated, any post can be reported in one click, and private messages only open after the other person accepts.

Will it be free?

Yes for members, with no ads and no selling of personal data. The plan is to be funded by schools, associations and companies that share opportunities and events.

Is this the real platform?

Not yet. This is a mockup presented at womENcourage 2026. Leave your email and we'll tell you when it launches.
